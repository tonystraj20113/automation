## Narration formula (from transcripts)

Both channels open **in medias res** with a vivid concrete image, not a
date-and-summary lead:
- "He saw gaps in a railroad every 39 feet."
- "In this world, a game of hide-and-seek started 400 years ago..."
- "In this town, there's only one rule when the carnival comes."

Recurring devices:
- **"You see,"** as the pivot from hook into explanation.
- Named people with a goal and a problem ("the youngest engineer", "his boss", "Mia").
- Concrete numbers and sensory detail: "finger-wide gap", "39 feet", "cost him everything", "voice like a squeaky door".
- Escalating repetition: "The next morning... The next morning..."
- Short dialogue lines, quoted directly.
- Causes explained inside the action ("a cart is fastest at the bottom of a loop").
- **Cliffhanger endings**, no tidy moral.
- `[music]` beds throughout — never bare voice.

Word count: Ricks Clips ~220 words / 63s (~210 wpm). Gneef ~600–720 words / 135–160s (~260 wpm).

## Rules adopted

1. Hook = one strange, specific image in the first sentence. Never "In 1900, X happened."
2. Second sentence starts with "You see," and grounds the world.
3. Every beat adds a new concrete detail (number, object, name, sound).
4. Use short sentences and fragments. "No flag. No men. Nobody answered."
5. End on an unresolved image, not a summary.
6. 55–80s for fact Shorts; visually 12–18 beats with a strong move each.
7. Always mix a low audio bed + transition SFX under narration.
